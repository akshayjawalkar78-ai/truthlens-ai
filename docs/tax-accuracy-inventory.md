# FirstFile tax accuracy inventory

**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-21
**Scope:** High-impact claims in the checklist, glossary, quiz, paycheck model, W-2 learning flow, and official-resource library. This inventory is educational maintenance guidance, not a substitute for checking current IRS instructions.

| Topic / claim | Current app treatment | Tax year | Verification and source | Conditions / maintenance note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Filing requirements | The checklist directs students to check current filing guidance; it does not make a universal teen-income determination. | 2025 returns; verify the year being filed | [IRS: Check if you need to file a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/check-if-you-need-to-file-a-tax-return); [Publication 501 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501) | Filing requirements depend on filing status, age, earned and unearned income, dependent status, self-employment, and special situations. Do not turn a single threshold into a rule for every teenager. |
| Dependent status and filing | The learning content explains that being claimed as a dependent does not itself prevent filing. | 2025 returns; verify the year being filed | [Publication 501 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501) | A dependent can have a filing requirement or may file to claim a refund. The dependent filing table and other circumstances matter. |
| 2024 W-2 quiz example | A fictional 17-year-old with $3,200 of W-2 wages, no other income, and no special filing triggers generally would not be required to file based on those facts alone; filing may claim federal withholding back. | 2024 | [IRS Publication 501 (2024), PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/p501--2024.pdf) | Keep the tax year and assumptions visible. Other income or special rules can change the result. |
| Self-employment filing and tax | Content now distinguishes $400 of net earnings from gross payments and explains that the 15.3% Social Security and Medicare rate generally applies to 92.35% of net earnings. Income tax is separate. | General concept; tax limits change annually | [IRS Topic No. 554: Self-Employment Tax](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554); [IRS Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) | The $400 threshold is generally based on net earnings. Exceptions and special rules can apply; do not use the 15.3% rate directly on gross receipts. |
| Refunds and withholding | The quiz now describes a refund as payments, including withholding, and refundable credits exceeding total tax. | General concept; return-specific | [IRS: Refunds](https://www.irs.gov/refunds) | A refund is not necessarily all paycheck withholding and is not the same as income earned or total tax. No refund is guaranteed. |
| W-2 | The app describes a W-2 as reporting wages and certain taxes withheld and labels its document example fictional. | General form purpose; use the current form/instructions | [IRS: About Form W-2](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-2) | Sample fields are educational examples, not a filing document. |
| 1099 forms and unreported income | The organizer says different 1099 forms report different things and that the form alone does not decide whether filing is required. | General concept; form-specific rules vary | [IRS: Get ready to file your taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-ready-to-file-your-taxes); [IRS Topic No. 554](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554) | Income may be reportable even when no information return arrives. Never treat all 1099 forms as interchangeable. |
| Employee FICA estimate | The paycheck tool uses a simplified employee-rate illustration of 6.2% Social Security plus 1.45% Medicare, and excludes wage-base limits and special cases. | Rates checked for 2026 | [IRS Topic No. 751: Social Security and Medicare Withholding Rates](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751) | The tool does not calculate federal or state income-tax withholding unless the user supplies it; it is not a full take-home-pay or annual-tax estimate. |
| IRS Free File | The checklist and resource copy no longer hard-code a universal AGI threshold; users are directed to check current program availability and provider terms. | Changes by filing season | [IRS Free File](https://www.irs.gov/e-file-do-your-taxes-for-free) | On 2026-10-21 the IRS page said Free File was closed and to check back in January 2027. Partner criteria and state-return availability vary. Do not reuse a season's AGI threshold as a permanent rule. |
| State income taxes | The questionnaire no longer preselects California for a new visitor. The app directs users to current state guidance rather than acting as a 50-state tax engine. | State- and year-specific | [IRS: Check if you need to file](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/check-if-you-need-to-file-a-tax-return) | State filing rules differ from federal rules. No Florida-specific filing determination is made by this app. |

## Verification notes

- External IRS resource links in `src/data/resources.js` were checked for HTTP 200 response and topic relevance on 2026-10-21. The Free File page was reachable but reported that the program was closed until January 2027.
- The CFPB homepage previously cited for paycheck guidance did not support the entry's specific source label. The glossary now links to IRS Topic 751, which supports the Social Security and Medicare withholding explanation.
- The W-2 checklist action now links directly to the Practice route; the old `?tab=w2-lab` parameter did not select a tab.
- A source was not treated as verified merely because it is linked from the app. Verify current-year rules again before adding thresholds, credits, or eligibility claims.
